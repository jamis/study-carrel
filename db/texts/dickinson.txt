section: 1
label: Success
1. Success is counted sweetest
By those who ne’er succeed.
To comprehend a nectar
Requires sorest need.
2. Not one of all the purple host
Who took the flag to-day
Can tell the definition,
So clear, of victory,
3. As he, defeated, dying,
On whose forbidden ear
The distant strains of triumph
Break, agonized and clear!

section: 2
label: Our share of night to bear
1. Our share of night to bear,
Our share of morning,
Our blank in bliss to fill,
Our blank in scorning.
2. Here a star, and there a star,
Some lose their way.
Here a mist, and there a mist,
Afterwards — day!

section: 3
label: Rouge et Noir
1. Soul, wilt thou toss again?
By just such a hazard
Hundreds have lost, indeed,
But tens have won an all.
2. Angels’ breathless ballot
Lingers to record thee;
Imps in eager caucus
Raffle for my soul.

section: 4
label: Rouge Gagne
1. ’T is so much joy! ’T is so much joy!
If I should fail, what poverty!
And yet, as poor as I
Have ventured all upon a throw;
Have gained! Yes! Hesitated so
This side the victory!
2. Life is but life, and death but death!
Bliss is but bliss, and breath but breath!
And if, indeed, I fail,
At least to know the worst is sweet.
Defeat means nothing but defeat,
No drearier can prevail!
3. And if I gain, — oh, gun at sea,
Oh, bells that in the steeples be,
At first repeat it slow!
For heaven is a different thing
Conjectured, and waked sudden in,
And might o’erwhelm me so!

section: 5
label: Glee! The great storm is over!
1. Glee! The great storm is over!
Four have recovered the land;
Forty gone down together
Into the boiling sand.
2. Ring, for the scant salvation!
Toll, for the bonnie souls, —
Neighbor and friend and bridegroom,
Spinning upon the shoals!
3. How they will tell the shipwreck
When winter shakes the door,
Till the children ask, “But the forty?
Did they come back no more?”
4. Then a silence suffuses the story,
And a softness the teller’s eye;
And the children no further question,
And only the waves reply.

section: 6
label: If I can stop one heart from breaking
1. If I can stop one heart from breaking,
I shall not live in vain;
If I can ease one life the aching,
Or cool one pain,
Or help one fainting robin
Unto his nest again,
I shall not live in vain.

section: 7
label: Almost!
1. Within my reach!
I could have touched!
I might have chanced that way!
Soft sauntered through the village,
Sauntered as soft away!
So unsuspected violets
Within the fields lie low,
Too late for striving fingers
That passed, an hour ago.

section: 8
label: A wounded deer leaps highest
1. A wounded deer leaps highest,
I’ve heard the hunter tell;
’T is but the ecstasy of death,
And then the brake is still.
2. The smitten rock that gushes,
The trampled steel that springs;
A cheek is always redder
Just where the hectic stings!
3. Mirth is the mail of anguish,
In which it cautions arm,
Lest anybody spy the blood
And “You’re hurt” exclaim!

section: 9
label: The heart asks pleasure first
1. The heart asks pleasure first,
And then, excuse from pain;
And then, those little anodynes
That deaden suffering;
2. And then, to go to sleep;
And then, if it should be
The will of its Inquisitor,
The liberty to die.

section: 10
label: In a Library
1. A precious, mouldering pleasure ’t is
To meet an antique book,
In just the dress his century wore;
A privilege, I think,
2. His venerable hand to take,
And warming in our own,
A passage back, or two, to make
To times when he was young.
3. His quaint opinions to inspect,
His knowledge to unfold
On what concerns our mutual mind,
The literature of old;
4. What interested scholars most,
What competitions ran
When Plato was a certainty.
And Sophocles a man;
5. When Sappho was a living girl,
And Beatrice wore
The gown that Dante deified.
Facts, centuries before,
6. He traverses familiar,
As one should come to town
And tell you all your dreams were true;
He lived where dreams were sown.
7. His presence is enchantment,
You beg him not to go;
Old volumes shake their vellum heads
And tantalize, just so.

section: 11
label: Much madness is divinest sense
1. Much madness is divinest sense
To a discerning eye;
Much sense the starkest madness.
’T is the majority
In this, as all, prevails.
Assent, and you are sane;
Demur, — you’re straightway dangerous,
And handled with a chain.
XII.
2. I asked no other thing,
No other was denied.
I offered Being for it;
The mighty merchant smiled.
3. Brazil? He twirled a button,
Without a glance my way:
“But, madam, is there nothing else
That we can show to-day?”

section: 12
label: Exclusion
1. The soul selects her own society,
Then shuts the door;
On her divine majority
Obtrude no more.
2. Unmoved, she notes the chariot’s pausing
At her low gate;
Unmoved, an emperor is kneeling
Upon her mat.
3. I’ve known her from an ample nation
Choose one;
Then close the valves of her attention
Like stone.

section: 13
label: The Secret
1. Some things that fly there be, —
Birds, hours, the bumble-bee:
Of these no elegy.
2. Some things that stay there be, —
Grief, hills, eternity:
Nor this behooveth me.
3. There are, that resting, rise.
Can I expound the skies?
How still the riddle lies!

section: 14
label: The Lonely House
1. I know some lonely houses off the road
A robber ’d like the look of, —
Wooden barred,
And windows hanging low,
Inviting to
A portico,
Where two could creep:
One hand the tools,
The other peep
To make sure all’s asleep.
Old-fashioned eyes,
Not easy to surprise!
2. How orderly the kitchen ’d look by night,
With just a clock, —
But they could gag the tick,
And mice won’t bark;
And so the walls don’t tell,
None will.
3. A pair of spectacles ajar just stir —
An almanac’s aware.
Was it the mat winked,
Or a nervous star?
The moon slides down the stair
To see who’s there.
4. There’s plunder, — where?
Tankard, or spoon,
Earring, or stone,
A watch, some ancient brooch
To match the grandmamma,
Staid sleeping there.
5. Day rattles, too,
Stealth’s slow;
The sun has got as far
As the third sycamore.
Screams chanticleer,
“Who’s there?”
And echoes, trains away,
Sneer — “Where?”
While the old couple, just astir,
Fancy the sunrise left the door ajar!

section: 15
label: To fight aloud is very brave
1. To fight aloud is very brave,
But gallanter, I know,
Who charge within the bosom,
The cavalry of woe.
2. Who win, and nations do not see,
Who fall, and none observe,
Whose dying eyes no country
Regards with patriot love.
3. We trust, in plumed procession,
For such the angels go,
Rank after rank, with even feet
And uniforms of snow.

section: 16
label: Dawn
1. When night is almost done,
And sunrise grows so near
That we can touch the spaces,
It ’s time to smooth the hair
2. And get the dimples ready,
And wonder we could care
For that old faded midnight
That frightened but an hour.

section: 17
label: The Book of Martyrs
1. Read, sweet, how others strove,
Till we are stouter;
What they renounced,
Till we are less afraid;
How many times they bore
The faithful witness,
Till we are helped,
As if a kingdom cared!
2. Read then of faith
That shone above the fagot;
Clear strains of hymn
The river could not drown;
Brave names of men
And celestial women,
Passed out of record
Into renown!

section: 18
label: The Mystery of Pain
1. Pain has an element of blank;
It cannot recollect
When it began, or if there were
A day when it was not.
2. It has no future but itself,
Its infinite realms contain
Its past, enlightened to perceive
New periods of pain.

section: 19
label: I taste a liquor never brewed
1. I taste a liquor never brewed,
From tankards scooped in pearl;
Not all the vats upon the Rhine
Yield such an alcohol!
2. Inebriate of air am I,
And debauchee of dew,
Reeling, through endless summer days,
From inns of molten blue.
3. When landlords turn the drunken bee
Out of the foxglove’s door,
When butterflies renounce their drams,
I shall but drink the more!
4. Till seraphs swing their snowy hats,
And saints to windows run,
To see the little tippler
Leaning against the sun!

section: 20
label: A Book
1. He ate and drank the precious words,
His spirit grew robust;
He knew no more that he was poor,
Nor that his frame was dust.
He danced along the dingy days,
And this bequest of wings
Was but a book. What liberty
A loosened spirit brings!

section: 21
label: I had no time to hate, because
1. I had no time to hate, because
The grave would hinder me,
And life was not so ample I
Could finish enmity.
2. Nor had I time to love; but since
Some industry must be,
The little toil of love, I thought,
Was large enough for me.

section: 22
label: Unreturning
1. ’T was such a little, little boat
That toddled down the bay!
’T was such a gallant, gallant sea
That beckoned it away!
2. ’T was such a greedy, greedy wave
That licked it from the coast;
Nor ever guessed the stately sails
My little craft was lost!

section: 23
label: Whether my bark went down at sea
1. Whether my bark went down at sea,
Whether she met with gales,
Whether to isles enchanted
She bent her docile sails;
2. By what mystic mooring
She is held to-day, —
This is the errand of the eye
Out upon the bay.

section: 24
label: Belshazzar had a letter
1. Belshazzar had a letter, —
He never had but one;
Belshazzar’s correspondent
Concluded and begun
In that immortal copy
The conscience of us all
Can read without its glasses
On revelation’s wall.

section: 25
label: The brain within its groove
1. The brain within its groove
Runs evenly and true;
But let a splinter swerve,
’T were easier for you
To put the water back
When floods have slit the hills,
And scooped a turnpike for themselves,
And blotted out the mills!

section: 26
label: Mine
1. Mine by the right of the white election!
Mine by the royal seal!
Mine by the sign in the scarlet prison
Bars cannot conceal!
2. Mine, here in vision and in veto!
Mine, by the grave’s repeal
Titled, confirmed, — delirious charter!
Mine, while the ages steal!

section: 27
label: Bequest
1. You left me, sweet, two legacies, —
A legacy of love
A Heavenly Father would content,
Had He the offer of;
2. You left me boundaries of pain
Capacious as the sea,
Between eternity and time,
Your consciousness and me.

section: 28
label: Alter? When the hills do.
1. Alter? When the hills do.
Falter? When the sun
Question if his glory
Be the perfect one.
2. Surfeit? When the daffodil
Doth of the dew:
Even as herself, O friend!
I will of you!

section: 29
label: Suspense
1. Elysium is as far as to
The very nearest room,
If in that room a friend await
Felicity or doom.
2. What fortitude the soul contains,
That it can so endure
The accent of a coming foot,
The opening of a door!

section: 30
label: Surrender
1. Doubt me, my dim companion!
Why, God would be content
With but a fraction of the love
Poured thee without a stint.
The whole of me, forever,
What more the woman can, —
Say quick, that I may dower thee
With last delight I own!
2. It cannot be my spirit,
For that was thine before;
I ceded all of dust I knew, —
What opulence the more
Had I, a humble maiden,
Whose farthest of degree
Was that she might,
Some distant heaven,
Dwell timidly with thee!

section: 31
label: If you were coming in the fall
1. If you were coming in the fall,
I’d brush the summer by
With half a smile and half a spurn,
As housewives do a fly.
2. If I could see you in a year,
I’d wind the months in balls,
And put them each in separate drawers,
Until their time befalls.
3. If only centuries delayed,
I’d count them on my hand,
Subtracting till my fingers dropped
Into Van Diemen’s land.
4. If certain, when this life was out,
That yours and mine should be,
I’d toss it yonder like a rind,
And taste eternity.
5. But now, all ignorant of the length
Of time’s uncertain wing,
It goads me, like the goblin bee,
That will not state its sting.

section: 32
label: With a Flower
1. I hide myself within my flower,
That wearing on your breast,
You, unsuspecting, wear me too —
And angels know the rest.
2. I hide myself within my flower,
That, fading from your vase,
You, unsuspecting, feel for me
Almost a loneliness.

section: 33
label: Proof
1. That I did always love,
I bring thee proof:
That till I loved
I did not love enough.
2. That I shall love alway,
I offer thee
That love is life,
And life hath immortality.
3. This, dost thou doubt, sweet?
Then have I
Nothing to show
But Calvary.

section: 34
label: Have you got a brook in your little heart
1. Have you got a brook in your little heart,
Where bashful flowers blow,
And blushing birds go down to drink,
And shadows tremble so?
2. And nobody knows, so still it flows,
That any brook is there;
And yet your little draught of life
Is daily drunken there.
3. Then look out for the little brook in March,
When the rivers overflow,
And the snows come hurrying from the hills,
And the bridges often go.
4. And later, in August it may be,
When the meadows parching lie,
Beware, lest this little brook of life
Some burning noon go dry!

section: 35
label: Transplanted
1. As if some little Arctic flower,
Upon the polar hem,
Went wandering down the latitudes,
Until it puzzled came
To continents of summer,
To firmaments of sun,
To strange, bright crowds of flowers,
And birds of foreign tongue!
I say, as if this little flower
To Eden wandered in —
What then? Why, nothing, only,
Your inference therefrom!

section: 36
label: The Outlet
1. My river runs to thee:
Blue sea, wilt welcome me?
2. My river waits reply.
Oh sea, look graciously!
3. I’ll fetch thee brooks
From spotted nooks, —
4. Say, sea,
Take me!

section: 37
label: In Vain
1. I cannot live with you,
It would be life,
And life is over there
Behind the shelf
2. The sexton keeps the key to,
Putting up
Our life, his porcelain,
Like a cup
3. Discarded of the housewife,
Quaint or broken;
A newer Sevres pleases,
Old ones crack.
4. I could not die with you,
For one must wait
To shut the other’s gaze down, —
You could not.
5. And I, could I stand by
And see you freeze,
Without my right of frost,
Death’s privilege?
6. Nor could I rise with you,
Because your face
Would put out Jesus’,
That new grace
7. Glow plain and foreign
On my homesick eye,
Except that you, than he
Shone closer by.
8. They’d judge us — how?
For you served Heaven, you know,
Or sought to;
I could not,
9. Because you saturated sight,
And I had no more eyes
For sordid excellence
As Paradise.
10. And were you lost, I would be,
Though my name
Rang loudest
On the heavenly fame.
11. And were you saved,
And I condemned to be
Where you were not,
That self were hell to me.
12. So we must keep apart,
You there, I here,
With just the door ajar
That oceans are,
And prayer,
And that pale sustenance,
Despair!

section: 38
label: Renunciation
1. There came a day at summer’s full
Entirely for me;
I thought that such were for the saints,
Where revelations be.
2. The sun, as common, went abroad,
The flowers, accustomed, blew,
As if no soul the solstice passed
That maketh all things new.
3. The time was scarce profaned by speech;
The symbol of a word
Was needless, as at sacrament
The wardrobe of our Lord.
4. Each was to each the sealed church,
Permitted to commune this time,
Lest we too awkward show
At supper of the Lamb.
5. The hours slid fast, as hours will,
Clutched tight by greedy hands;
So faces on two decks look back,
Bound to opposing lands.
6. And so, when all the time had failed,
Without external sound,
Each bound the other’s crucifix,
We gave no other bond.
7. Sufficient troth that we shall rise —
Deposed, at length, the grave —
To that new marriage, justified
Through Calvaries of Love!

section: 39
label: Love’s Baptism
1. I’m ceded, I’ve stopped being theirs;
The name they dropped upon my face
With water, in the country church,
Is finished using now,
And they can put it with my dolls,
My childhood, and the string of spools
I’ve finished threading too.
2. Baptized before without the choice,
But this time consciously, of grace
Unto supremest name,
Called to my full, the crescent dropped,
Existence’s whole arc filled up
With one small diadem.
3. My second rank, too small the first,
Crowned, crowing on my father’s breast,
A half unconscious queen;
But this time, adequate, erect,
With will to choose or to reject.
And I choose — just a throne.

section: 40
label: Resurrection
1. ’T was a long parting, but the time
For interview had come;
Before the judgment-seat of God,
The last and second time
2. These fleshless lovers met,
A heaven in a gaze,
A heaven of heavens, the privilege
Of one another’s eyes.
3. No lifetime set on them,
Apparelled as the new
Unborn, except they had beheld,
Born everlasting now.
4. Was bridal e’er like this?
A paradise, the host,
And cherubim and seraphim
The most familiar guest.

section: 41
label: Apocalypse
1. I’m wife; I’ve finished that,
That other state;
I’m Czar, I’m woman now:
It’s safer so.
2. How odd the girl’s life looks
Behind this soft eclipse!
I think that earth seems so
To those in heaven now.
3. This being comfort, then
That other kind was pain;
But why compare?
I’m wife! stop there!

section: 42
label: The Wife
1. She rose to his requirement, dropped
The playthings of her life
To take the honorable work
Of woman and of wife.
2. If aught she missed in her new day
Of amplitude, or awe,
Or first prospective, or the gold
In using wore away,
3. It lay unmentioned, as the sea
Develops pearl and weed,
But only to himself is known
The fathoms they abide.

section: 43
label: Apotheosis
1. Come slowly, Eden!
Lips unused to thee,
Bashful, sip thy jasmines,
As the fainting bee,
2. Reaching late his flower,
Round her chamber hums,
Counts his nectars — enters,
And is lost in balms!

section: 44
label: May-flower
1. Pink, small, and punctual,
Aromatic, low,
Covert in April,
Candid in May,
2. Dear to the moss,
Known by the knoll,
Next to the robin
In every human soul.
3. Bold little beauty,
Bedecked with thee,
Nature forswears
Antiquity.

section: 45
label: Why?
1. The murmur of a bee
A witchcraft yieldeth me.
If any ask me why,
’T were easier to die
Than tell.
2. The red upon the hill
Taketh away my will;
If anybody sneer,
Take care, for God is here,
That’s all.
3. The breaking of the day
Addeth to my degree;
If any ask me how,
Artist, who drew me so,
Must tell!

section: 46
label: Perhaps you’d like to buy a flower?
1. Perhaps you’d like to buy a flower?
But I could never sell.
If you would like to borrow
Until the daffodil
2. Unties her yellow bonnet
Beneath the village door,
Until the bees, from clover rows
Their hock and sherry draw,
3. Why, I will lend until just then,
But not an hour more!

section: 47
label: The pedigree of honey
1. The pedigree of honey
Does not concern the bee;
A clover, any time, to him
Is aristocracy.

section: 48
label: A Service of Song
1. Some keep the Sabbath going to church;
I keep it staying at home,
With a bobolink for a chorister,
And an orchard for a dome.
2. Some keep the Sabbath in surplice;
I just wear my wings,
And instead of tolling the bell for church,
Our little sexton sings.
3. God preaches, — a noted clergyman, — 
And the sermon is never long;
So instead of getting to heaven at last,
I’m going all along!

section: 49
label: The bee is not afraid of me
1. The bee is not afraid of me,
I know the butterfly;
The pretty people in the woods
Receive me cordially.
2. The brooks laugh louder when I come,
The breezes madder play.
Wherefore, mine eyes, thy silver mists?
Wherefore, O summer’s day?

section: 50
label: Summer’s Armies
1. Some rainbow coming from the fair!
Some vision of the world Cashmere
I confidently see!
Or else a peacock’s purple train,
Feather by feather, on the plain
Fritters itself away!
2. The dreamy butterflies bestir,
Lethargic pools resume the whir
Of last year’s sundered tune.
From some old fortress on the sun
Baronial bees march, one by one,
In murmuring platoon!
3. The robins stand as thick to-day
As flakes of snow stood yesterday,
On fence and roof and twig.
The orchis binds her feather on
For her old lover, Don the Sun,
Revisiting the bog!
4. Without commander, countless, still,
The regiment of wood and hill
In bright detachment stand.
Behold! Whose multitudes are these?
The children of whose turbaned seas,
Or what Circassian land?

section: 51
label: The Grass
1. The grass so little has to do, —
A sphere of simple green,
With only butterflies to brood,
And bees to entertain,
2. And stir all day to pretty tunes
The breezes fetch along,
And hold the sunshine in its lap
And bow to everything;
3. And thread the dews all night, like pearls,
And make itself so fine, —
A duchess were too common
For such a noticing.
4. And even when it dies, to pass
In odors so divine,
As lowly spices gone to sleep,
Or amulets of pine.
5. And then to dwell in sovereign barns,
And dream the days away, —
The grass so little has to do,
I wish I were the hay!

section: 52
label: A little road not made of man
1. A little road not made of man,
Enabled of the eye,
Accessible to thill of bee,
Or cart of butterfly.
2. If town it have, beyond itself,
’T is that I cannot say;
I only sigh, — no vehicle
Bears me along that way.

section: 53
label: Summer Shower
1. A drop fell on the apple tree,
Another on the roof;
A half a dozen kissed the eaves,
And made the gables laugh.
2. A few went out to help the brook,
That went to help the sea.
Myself conjectured, Were they pearls,
What necklaces could be!
3. The dust replaced in hoisted roads,
The birds jocoser sung;
The sunshine threw his hat away,
The orchards spangles hung.
4. The breezes brought dejected lutes,
And bathed them in the glee;
The East put out a single flag,
And signed the fete away.

section: 54
label: Psalm of the Day
1. A something in a summer’s day,
As slow her flambeaux burn away,
Which solemnizes me.
2. A something in a summer’s noon, —
An azure depth, a wordless tune,
Transcending ecstasy.
3. And still within a summer’s night
A something so transporting bright,
I clap my hands to see;
4. Then veil my too inspecting face,
Lest such a subtle, shimmering grace
Flutter too far for me.
5. The wizard-fingers never rest,
The purple brook within the breast
Still chafes its narrow bed;
6. Still rears the East her amber flag,
Guides still the sun along the crag
His caravan of red,
7. Like flowers that heard the tale of dews,
But never deemed the dripping prize
Awaited their low brows;
8. Or bees, that thought the summer’s name
Some rumor of delirium
No summer could for them;
9. Or Arctic creature, dimly stirred
By tropic hint, — some travelled bird
Imported to the wood;
10. Or wind’s bright signal to the ear,
Making that homely and severe,
Contented, known, before
11. The heaven unexpected came,
To lives that thought their worshipping
A too presumptuous psalm.

section: 55
label: The Sea of Sunset
1. This is the land the sunset washes,
These are the banks of the Yellow Sea;
Where it rose, or whither it rushes,
These are the western mystery!
2. Night after night her purple traffic
Strews the landing with opal bales;
Merchantmen poise upon horizons,
Dip, and vanish with fairy sails.

section: 56
label: Purple Clover
1. There is a flower that bees prefer,
And butterflies desire;
To gain the purple democrat
The humming-birds aspire.
2. And whatsoever insect pass,
A honey bears away
Proportioned to his several dearth
And her capacity.
3. Her face is rounder than the moon,
And ruddier than the gown
Of orchis in the pasture,
Or rhododendron worn.
4. She doth not wait for June;
Before the world is green
Her sturdy little countenance
Against the wind is seen,
5. Contending with the grass,
Near kinsman to herself,
For privilege of sod and sun,
Sweet litigants for life.
6. And when the hills are full,
And newer fashions blow,
Doth not retract a single spice
For pang of jealousy.
7. Her public is the noon,
Her providence the sun,
Her progress by the bee proclaimed
In sovereign, swerveless tune.
8. The bravest of the host,
Surrendering the last,
Nor even of defeat aware
When cancelled by the frost.

section: 57
label: The Bee
1. Like trains of cars on tracks of plush
I hear the level bee:
A jar across the flowers goes,
Their velvet masonry
2. Withstands until the sweet assault
Their chivalry consumes,

To vanquish other blooms.

His helmet is of gold;

With chrysoprase, inlaid.

His idleness a tune;
Oh, for a bee’s experience
Of clovers and of noon!

Indicative that suns go down;
The notice to the startled grass
That darkness is about to pass.

My flowers raise their pretty lips,
Then put their nightgowns on.

Merry that it is morn,
My flowers from a hundred cribs
Will peep, and prance again.

May be seen the dews among,
Stooping, plucking, smiling, flying:

Stooping, plucking, sighing, flying;

Her simple haunts beyond!

For whom betrayed the dell,

The seasons fit the same,

On deserts, in the sky,
Had broken rank,

To any happy flower,
The frost beheads it at its play
In accidental power.

The sun proceeds unmoved
To measure off another day
For an approving God.

Than when the cricket came,

Meant nought but going home.

To take a backward look.

Almost thy plausibility
Induces my belief,

Hurries a timid leaf!

Thy consecrated bread to break,
Taste thine immortal wine!

The rose is out of town.

The field a scarlet gown.

I’ll put a trinket on.

Debates if it will go.

How some one treated him;
Nature, like us, is sometimes caught
Without her diadem.

section: 72

Upon a marge of snow;
It suits his own austerity,
And satisfies an awe
2. That men must slake in wilderness,
Or in the desert cloy, —
An instinct for the hoar, the bald,
Lapland’s necessity.

The gnash of northern winds

His best Norwegian wines.

Beneath his tabernacles play,
And Dnieper wrestlers run.

On winter afternoons,

Of cathedral tunes.

Where the meanings are.

’T is the seal, despair, —

Sent us of the air.

Shadows hold their breath;
When it goes, ’t is like the distance
On the look of death.

section: 74

Her loving bosom lay.

Later by just an hour than death, —
Oh, lagging yesterday!

Were undefeated still?

That could not stop to be a king,
Doubtful if it be crowned!

Creation looking on.

Rafter of satin, and roof of stone.

Horizons straggled down.

On whom the summer shone!

No daybreak can bestir;
The slow archangel’s syllables
Must awaken her.

Degrades the dress I wear.

And cipher at the sign,

I take the clew divine.

Into deep eternity!

The divine intoxication
Of the first league out from land?

section: 91

At the drowsiest?

Made nature different.

Italicized, as ’t were.

Within my income these could lie,
And life and I keep even.

It would suffice my prayer
But just for one to stipulate,
And grace would grant the pair.

A heaven not so large as yours,
But large enough for me.

The quiet ages picked it up,

As take the tale for true
That “Whatsoever you shall ask,
Itself be given you.”

With a suspicious air, —
As children, swindled for the first,
All swindlers be, infer.

Or too impetuous bird.

And infiniter care,
Her golden finger on her lip,
Wills silence everywhere.

Whom I have never found

I left them in the ground.

I tried to match it, seam by seam,
But could not make them fit.
2. The thought behind I strove to join
Unto the thought before,
But sequence ravelled out of reach
Like balls upon a floor.

section: 302
label: Reticence
1. The reticent volcano keeps
His never slumbering plan;
Confided are his projects pink
To no precarious man.
2. If nature will not tell the tale
Jehovah told to her,
Can human nature not survive
Without a listener?
3. Admonished by her buckled lips
Let every babbler be.
The only secret people keep
Is Immortality.

section: 303
label: With Flowers
1. If recollecting were forgetting,
Then I remember not;
And if forgetting, recollecting,
How near I had forgot!
And if to miss were merry,
And if to mourn were gay,
How very blithe the fingers
That gathered these to-day!

section: 304
label: The farthest thunder that I heard
1. The farthest thunder that I heard
Was nearer than the sky,
And rumbles still, though torrid noons
Have lain their missiles by.
The lightning that preceded it
Struck no one but myself,
But I would not exchange the bolt
For all the rest of life.
Indebtedness to oxygen
The chemist may repay,
But not the obligation
To electricity.
It founds the homes and decks the days,
And every clamor bright
Is but the gleam concomitant
Of that waylaying light.
The thought is quiet as a flake, —
A crash without a sound;
How life’s reverberation
Its explanation found!

section: 305
label: On the bleakness of my lot
1. On the bleakness of my lot
Bloom I strove to raise.
Late, my acre of a rock
Yielded grape and maize.
2. Soil of flint if steadfast tilled
Will reward the hand;
Seed of palm by Lybian sun
Fructified in sand.

section: 306

1. A door just opened on a street —
I, lost, was passing by —
An instant’s width of warmth disclosed,
And wealth, and company.
2. The door as sudden shut, and I,
I, lost, was passing by, —
Lost doubly, but by contrast most,
Enlightening misery.

section: 307
label: Friends
1. Are friends delight or pain?
Could bounty but remain
Riches were good.
2. But if they only stay
Bolder to fly away,
Riches are sad.

section: 308

1. Ashes denote that fire was;
Respect the grayest pile
For the departed creature’s sake
That hovered there awhile.
2. Fire exists the first in light,
And then consolidates, —
Only the chemist can disclose
Into what carbonates.

section: 309
label: A Man
1. Fate slew him, but he did not drop;
She felled — he did not fall — 
Impaled him on her fiercest stakes —
He neutralized them all.
2. She stung him, sapped his firm advance,
But, when her worst was done,
And he, unmoved, regarded her,
Acknowledged him a man.

section: 310
label: Ventures
1. Finite to fail, but infinite to venture.
For the one ship that struts the shore
Many’s the gallant, overwhelmed creature
Nodding in navies nevermore.

section: 311
label: Griefs
1. I measure every grief I meet
With analytic eyes;
I wonder if it weighs like mine,
Or has an easier size.
2. I wonder if they bore it long,
Or did it just begin?
I could not tell the date of mine,
It feels so old a pain.
3. I wonder if it hurts to live,
And if they have to try,
And whether, could they choose between,
They would not rather die.
4. I wonder if when years have piled —
Some thousands — on the cause
Of early hurt, if such a lapse
Could give them any pause;
5. Or would they go on aching still
Through centuries above,
Enlightened to a larger pain
By contrast with the love.
6. The grieved are many, I am told;
The reason deeper lies, —
Death is but one and comes but once,
And only nails the eyes.
7. There’s grief of want, and grief of cold, —
A sort they call ’despair;’
There’s banishment from native eyes,
In sight of native air.
8. And though I may not guess the kind
Correctly, yet to me
A piercing comfort it affords
In passing Calvary,
9. To note the fashions of the cross,
Of those that stand alone,
Still fascinated to presume
That some are like my own.

section: 312
label: I have a king who does not speak
1. I have a king who does not speak;
So, wondering, thro’ the hours meek
I trudge the day away,—
Half glad when it is night and sleep,
If, haply, thro’ a dream to peep
In parlors shut by day.
2. And if I do, when morning comes,
It is as if a hundred drums
Did round my pillow roll,
And shouts fill all my childish sky,
And bells keep saying ’victory’
From steeples in my soul!
3. And if I don’t, the little Bird
Within the Orchard is not heard,
And I omit to pray,
’Father, thy will be done’ to-day,
For my will goes the other way,
And it were perjury!

section: 313
label: Disenchantment
1. It dropped so low in my regard
I heard it hit the ground,
And go to pieces on the stones
At bottom of my mind;
2. Yet blamed the fate that fractured, less
Than I reviled myself
For entertaining plated wares
Upon my silver shelf.

section: 314

1. To lose one’s faith surpasses
The loss of an estate,
Because estates can be
Replenished, — faith cannot.
2. Inherited with life,
Belief but once can be;
Annihilate a single clause,
And Being’s beggary.

section: 315

1. I had a daily bliss
I half indifferent viewed,
Till sudden I perceived it stir, —
It grew as I pursued,
2. Till when, around a crag,
It wasted from my sight,
Enlarged beyond my utmost scope,
I learned its sweetness right.

1. I worked for chaff, and earning wheat
Was haughty and betrayed.
What right had fields to arbitrate
In matters ratified?
2. I tasted wheat, — and hated chaff,
And thanked the ample friend;
Wisdom is more becoming viewed
At distance than at hand.

section: 317

1. Life, and Death, and Giants
Such as these, are still.
Minor apparatus, hopper of the mill,
Beetle at the candle,
Or a fife’s small fame,
Maintain by accident
That they proclaim.

So still, so cool,
Till, some odd afternoon,
The Alps neglect their curtains,
And we look farther on.
2. Italy stands the other side,
While, like a guard between,
The solemn Alps,
The siren Alps,
Forever intervene!

section: 319

’T is something like a house;
It has a garret also
For refuse and the mouse,
2. Besides, the deepest cellar
That ever mason hewed;
Look to it, by its fathoms
Ourselves be not pursued.

section: 320

1. To hang our head ostensibly,
And subsequent to find
That such was not the posture
Of our immortal mind,
2. Affords the sly presumption
That, in so dense a fuzz,
You, too, take cobweb attitudes
Upon a plane of gauze!

section: 321
label: The Brain
1. The brain is wider than the sky,
For, put them side by side,
The one the other will include
With ease, and you beside.
2. The brain is deeper than the sea,
For, hold them, blue to blue,
The one the other will absorb,
As sponges, buckets do.
3. The brain is just the weight of God,
For, lift them, pound for pound,
And they will differ, if they do,
As syllable from sound.

section: 322

1. The bone that has no marrow;
What ultimate for that?
It is not fit for table,
For beggar, or for cat.
2. A bone has obligations,
A being has the same;
A marrowless assembly
Is culpabler than shame.
3. But how shall finished creatures
A function fresh obtain? —
Old Nicodemus’ phantom
Confronting us again!

section: 323

1. The past is such a curious creature,
To look her in the face
A transport may reward us,
Or a disgrace.
2. Unarmed if any meet her,
I charge him, fly!
Her rusty ammunition
Might yet reply!

section: 324

1. To help our bleaker parts
Salubrious hours are given,
Which if they do not fit for earth
Drill silently for heaven.

section: 325

1. What soft, cherubic creatures
These gentlewomen are!
One would as soon assault a plush
Or violate a star.
2. Such dimity convictions,
A horror so refined
Of freckled human nature,
Of Deity ashamed, —
3. It’s such a common glory,
A fisherman’s degree!
Redemption, brittle lady,
Be so, ashamed of thee.

section: 326

Remains to him unknown:
The banquet of abstemiousness
Surpasses that of wine.
2. Within its hope, though yet ungrasped
Desire’s perfect goal,
No nearer, lest reality
Should disenthrall thy soul.

section: 327

1. It might be easier
To fail with land in sight,
Than gain my blue peninsula
To perish of delight.

section: 328

1. You cannot put a fire out;
A thing that can ignite
Can go, itself, without a fan
Upon the slowest night.
2. You cannot fold a flood
And put it in a drawer, —
Because the winds would find it out,
And tell your cedar floor.

1. A modest lot, a fame petite,
A brief campaign of sting and sweet
Is plenty! Is enough!
A sailor’s business is the shore,
A soldier’s — balls. Who asketh more
Must seek the neighboring life!

section: 330

1. Is bliss, then, such abyss
I must not put my foot amiss
For fear I spoil my shoe?
2. I’d rather suit my foot
Than save my boot,
For yet to buy another pair
Is possible
At any fair.
3. But bliss is sold just once;
The patent lost
None buy it any more.

section: 331
label: Experience
1. I stepped from plank to plank
So slow and cautiously;
The stars about my head I felt,
About my feet the sea.
2. I knew not but the next
Would be my final inch, —
This gave me that precarious gait
Some call experience.

section: 332
label: Thanksgiving Day
1. One day is there of the series
Termed Thanksgiving day,
Celebrated part at table,
Part in memory.
2. Neither patriarch nor pussy,
I dissect the play;
Seems it, to my hooded thinking,
Reflex holiday.
3. Had there been no sharp subtraction
From the early sum,
Not an acre or a caption
Where was once a room,
4. Not a mention, whose small pebble
Wrinkled any bay, —
Unto such, were such assembly,
’T were Thanksgiving day.

section: 333
label: Childish Griefs
1. Softened by Time’s consummate plush,
How sleek the woe appears
That threatened childhood’s citadel
And undermined the years!
2. Bisected now by bleaker griefs,
We envy the despair
That devastated childhood’s realm,
So easy to repair.

section: 334
label: Consecration
1. Proud of my broken heart since thou didst break it,
Proud of the pain I did not feel till thee,
Proud of my night since thou with moons dost slake it,
Not to partake thy passion, my humility.

section: 335

1. My worthiness is all my doubt,
His merit all my fear,
Contrasting which, my qualities
Do lowlier appear;
2. Lest I should insufficient prove
For his beloved need,
The chiefest apprehension
Within my loving creed.
3. So I, the undivine abode
Of his elect content,
Conform my soul as ’t were a church
Unto her sacrament.

section: 336
label: Love
1. Love is anterior to life,
Posterior to death,
Initial of creation, and
The exponent of breath.

section: 337
label: Satisfied
1. One blessing had I, than the rest
So larger to my eyes
That I stopped gauging, satisfied,
For this enchanted size.
2. It was the limit of my dream,
The focus of my prayer, —
A perfect, paralyzing bliss
Contented as despair.
3. I knew no more of want or cold,
Phantasms both become,
For this new value in the soul,
Supremest earthly sum.
4. The heaven below the heaven above
Obscured with ruddier hue.
Life’s latitude leant over-full;
The judgment perished, too.
5. Why joys so scantily disburse,
Why Paradise defer,
Why floods are served to us in bowls, —
I speculate no more.

1. When roses cease to bloom, dear,
And violets are done,
When bumble-bees in solemn flight
Have passed beyond the sun,
2. The hand that paused to gather
Upon this summer’s day
Will idle lie, in Auburn, —
Then take my flower, pray!

section: 339

When summer days are flown!
Thy music still when whippoorwill
And oriole are done!
2. For thee to bloom, I’ll skip the tomb
And sow my blossoms o’er!
Pray gather me, Anemone,
Thy flower forevermore!

Bulb after bulb, in silver rolled,
Scantily dealt to the summer morning,
Saved for your ear when lutes be old.
2. Loose the flood, you shall find it patent,
Gush after gush, reserved for you;
Scarlet experiment! sceptic Thomas,
Now, do you doubt that your bird was true?

All other hearts I knew.
’T is true the drought is destitute,
But then I had the dew!
2. The Caspian has its realms of sand,
Its other realm of sea;
Without the sterile perquisite
No Caspian could be.

Be debonair! Be debonair!

section: 343

Which bears a sword
Can pierce an armed man.
It hurls its barbed syllables,—
At once is mute again.

The saved will tell
On patriotic day,
Some epauletted brother
Gave his breath away.

Wherever roams the day,
There is its noiseless onset,
There is its victory!
3. Behold the keenest marksman!
The most accomplished shot!
Time’s sublimest target
Is a soul ’forgot’!

section: 344

1. I’ve got an arrow here;
Loving the hand that sent it,
I the dart revere.
2. Fell, they will say, in ’skirmish’!
Vanquished, my soul will know,
By but a simple arrow
Sped by an archer’s bow.

section: 345

As players at the keys
Before they drop full music on;
He stuns you by degrees,

For the ethereal blow,
By fainter hammers, further heard,
Then nearer, then so slow
3. Your breath has time to straighten,
Your brain to bubble cool, —
Deals one imperial thunderbolt
That scalps your naked soul.

You and I, to-night!
You may forget the warmth he gave,
I will forget the light.
2. When you have done, pray tell me,
That I my thoughts may dim;
Haste! lest while you’re lagging,
I may remember him!

That were the little load;
I bring thee the imperial heart
I had not strength to hold.
2. The heart I cherished in my own
Till mine too heavy grew,
Yet strangest, heavier since it went,
Is it too large for you?

And put it in the drawer,
Till it an antique fashion shows
Like costumes grandsires wore.

Because it flies!

Because it dies!
Barbs has it, like a bee.
Ah, curious friend,
Thou puzzlest me!

section: 351

1. He touched me, so I live to know
That such a day, permitted so,
I groped upon his breast.
It was a boundless place to me,
And silenced, as the awful sea
Puts minor streams to rest.
2. And now, I’m different from before,
As if I breathed superior air,
Or brushed a royal gown;
My feet, too, that had wandered so,
My gypsy face transfigured now
To tenderer renown.

section: 352

1. Let me not mar that perfect dream
By an auroral stain,
But so adjust my daily night
That it will come again.

section: 353
label: Numen Lumen
1. I live with him, I see his face;
I go no more away
For visitor, or sundown;
Death’s single privacy,
2. The only one forestalling mine,
And that by right that he
Presents a claim invisible,
No wedlock granted me.
3. I live with him, I hear his voice,
I stand alive to-day
To witness to the certainty
Of immortality
4. Taught me by Time, — the lower way,
Conviction every day, —
That life like this is endless,
Be judgment what it may.

section: 354
label: Longing
1. I envy seas whereon he rides,
I envy spokes of wheels
Of chariots that him convey,
I envy speechless hills
2. That gaze upon his journey;
How easy all can see
What is forbidden utterly
As heaven, unto me!
3. I envy nests of sparrows
That dot his distant eaves,
The wealthy fly upon his pane,
The happy, happy leaves
4. That just abroad his window
Have summer’s leave to be,
The earrings of Pizarro
Could not obtain for me.
5. I envy light that wakes him,
And bells that boldly ring
To tell him it is noon abroad, —
Myself his noon could bring,
6. Yet interdict my blossom
And abrogate my bee,
Lest noon in everlasting night
Drop Gabriel and me.

section: 355
label: Wedded
1. A solemn thing it was, I said,
A woman white to be,
And wear, if God should count me fit,
Her hallowed mystery.
2. A timid thing to drop a life
Into the purple well,
Too plummetless that it come back
Eternity until.

section: 356

1. The springtime’s pallid landscape
Will glow like bright bouquet,
Though drifted deep in parian
The village lies to-day.

With purple load will hang;

Their old forefathers sang.

And covenant gentians frill,

Or priests adjust the symbols
When sacrament is done.

section: 357

1. She slept beneath a tree
Remembered but by me.
I touched her cradle mute;
She recognized the foot,
Put on her carmine suit, —

section: 358
label: A light exists in spring
1. A light exists in spring
Not present on the year
At any other period.
When March is scarcely here
2. A color stands abroad
On solitary hills
That science cannot overtake,
But human nature feels.
3. It waits upon the lawn;
It shows the furthest tree
Upon the furthest slope we know;
It almost speaks to me.

Or noons report away,
Without the formula of sound,
It passes, and we stay:
5. A quality of loss
Affecting our content,
As trade had suddenly encroached
Upon a sacrament.

section: 359
label: The Waking Year
1. A lady red upon the hill
Her annual secret keeps;
A lady white within the field
In placid lily sleeps!
2. The tidy breezes with their brooms
Sweep vale, and hill, and tree!
Prithee, my pretty housewives!
Who may expected be?
3. The neighbors do not yet suspect!
The woods exchange a smile —
Orchard, and buttercup, and bird —
In such a little while!
4. And yet how still the landscape stands,
How nonchalant the wood,
As if the resurrection
Were nothing very odd!

section: 360

1. Dear March, come in!
How glad I am!
I looked for you before.
Put down your hat —
You must have walked —
How out of breath you are!

Lock the door!

When I am occupied.

As soon as you have come,

And praise as mere as blame.

section: 361

Bold it were to die
With the blue-birds buccaneering
On his British sky.

section: 362

I open every door;
Or has it feathers like a bird,
Or billows like a shore?

section: 363

1. A murmur in the trees to note,
Not loud enough for wind;
A star not far enough to seek,
Nor near enough to find;
2. A long, long yellow on the lawn,
A hubbub as of feet;
Not audible, as ours to us,
But dapperer, more sweet;

To houses unperceived, —

Would never be believed.

Although I heard them try!

How could I break my word?

No fear you’ll miss the road.

section: 364

1. Morning is the place for dew,
Corn is made at noon,
After dinner light for flowers,
Dukes for setting sun!

The bushes they were bells;
I could not find a privacy
From Nature’s sentinels.
2. In cave if I presumed to hide,

section: 366

1. A sepal, petal, and a thorn
Upon a common summer’s morn,
A flash of dew, a bee or two,
A breeze
A caper in the trees, —
And I’m a rose!

section: 367

He trod upon the trees
As he esteemed them trifles,

Nature had left behind.

Of a dependent brood;

His remedy for care, —
A contrast to our respites.
How different we are!

section: 368

Has never been employed
Though his surpassing merit
Is freely certified
2. By every broom and Bridget
Throughout a Christian land.
Neglected son of genius,
I take thee by the hand.

section: 369

1. What mystery pervades a well!
The water lives so far,
Like neighbor from another world
Residing in a jar.
2. The grass does not appear afraid;
I often wonder he
Can stand so close and look so bold
At what is dread to me.

No evidence gives he.

The ones that cite her most
Have never passed her haunted house,
Nor simplified her ghost.

Is helped by the regret
That those who know her, know her less
The nearer her they get.

At that enthralling gallop

A snake is summer’s treason,

section: 375

1. Could I but ride indefinite,
As doth the meadow-bee,
And visit only where I liked,
And no man visit me,
2. And flirt all day with buttercups,
And marry whom I may,
And dwell a little everywhere,
Or better, run away
3. With no police to follow,
Or chase me if I do,
Till I should jump peninsulas
To get away from you, —
4. I said, but just to be a bee
Upon a raft of air,
And row in nowhere all day long,
And anchor off the bar,—
What liberty! So captives deem
Who tight in dungeons are.

section: 376
label: The Moon
1. The moon was but a chin of gold
A night or two ago,
And now she turns her perfect face
Upon the world below.
2. Her forehead is of amplest blond;
Her cheek like beryl stone;
Her eye unto the summer dew
The likest I have known.
3. Her lips of amber never part;
But what must be the smile
Upon her friend she could bestow
Were such her silver will!
4. And what a privilege to be
But the remotest star!
For certainly her way might pass
Beside your twinkling door.
5. Her bonnet is the firmament,
The universe her shoe,
The stars the trinkets at her belt,
Her dimities of blue.

section: 377
label: The Bat
1. The bat is dun with wrinkled wings
Like fallow article,
And not a song pervades his lips,
Or none perceptible.
2. His small umbrella, quaintly halved,
Describing in the air
An arc alike inscrutable, —
Elate philosopher!
3. Deputed from what firmament
Of what astute abode,
Empowered with what malevolence
Auspiciously withheld.
4. To his adroit Creator
Ascribe no less the praise;
Beneficent, believe me,
His eccentricities.

section: 378
label: The Balloon
1. You’ve seen balloons set, haven’t you?
So stately they ascend
It is as swans discarded you
For duties diamond.
2. Their liquid feet go softly out
Upon a sea of blond;
They spurn the air as ’t were too mean
For creatures so renowned.
3. Their ribbons just beyond the eye,
They struggle some for breath,
And yet the crowd applauds below;
They would not encore death.
4. The gilded creature strains and spins,
Trips frantic in a tree,
Tears open her imperial veins
And tumbles in the sea.
5. The crowd retire with an oath
The dust in streets goes down,
And clerks in counting-rooms observe,
’’T was only a balloon.’

section: 379

1. The cricket sang,
And set the sun,

Their seam the day upon.

The twilight stood as strangers do
With hat in hand, polite and new,
To stay as if, or go.
3. A vastness, as a neighbor, came, —
A wisdom without face or name,
A peace, as hemispheres at home, —
And so the night became.

section: 382
label: Aurora
1. Of bronze and blaze
The north, to-night!
So adequate its forms,
So preconcerted with itself,
So distant to alarms, —

To universe, or me,
It paints my simple spirit
With tints of majesty,

And strut upon my stem,
Disdaining men and oxygen,
For arrogance of them.
2. My splendors are menagerie;
But their competeless show
Will entertain the centuries
When I am, long ago,
An island in dishonored grass,
Whom none but daisies know.

section: 383
label: The Coming of Night
1. How the old mountains drip with sunset,
And the brake of dun!
How the hemlocks are tipped in tinsel
By the wizard sun!
2. How the old steeples hand the scarlet,
Till the ball is full, —
Have I the lip of the flamingo
That I dare to tell?
3. Then, how the fire ebbs like billows,
Touching all the grass
With a departing, sapphire feature,
As if a duchess pass!
4. How a small dusk crawls on the village
Till the houses blot;
And the odd flambeaux no men carry
Glimmer on the spot!
5. Now it is night in nest and kennel,
And where was the wood,
Just a dome of abyss is nodding
Into solitude! —

Titian never told;
Domenichino dropped the pencil,
Powerless to unfold.

But murmuring of some
Posterior, prophetic,
Has simultaneous come, —

When nature’s laugh is done, —
The Revelations of the book
Whose Genesis is June.

How vast an one
Was recently among us.
A perished sun

How doubly more
Than all the golden presence
It was before!

Oh, thou celestial host!
Bride of the Father and the Son,
Bride of the Holy Ghost!

Wedlock of will decay;

Conquers mortality.

The tranquiller to die;
That such have lived, certificate
For immortality.

section: 392

When I forget to tease,
They’ll recollect how cold I looked,
And how I just said ’please.’

To call the little child,
Who cannot thank them, for the ice
That on her lisping piled.

section: 393

And makes one lift one’s hat,
As one encountered gentlefolk
Upon a daily street,

Though pyramids decay,
And kingdoms, like the orchard,
Flit russetly away.

section: 394

1. The distance that the dead have gone
Does not at first appear;
Their coming back seems possible
For many an ardent year.
2. And then, that we have followed them
We more than half suspect,
So intimate have we become
With their dear retrospect.

section: 395
label: How dare the robins sing
1. How dare the robins sing,
When men and women hear
Who since they went to their account
Have settled with the year! —
Paid all that life had earned
In one consummate bill,
And now, what life or death can do
Is immaterial.
Insulting is the sun
To him whose mortal light,
Beguiled of immortality,
Bequeaths him to the night.
In deference to him
Extinct be every hum,
Whose garden wrestles with the dew,
At daybreak overcome!

section: 396
label: Death
1. Death is like the insect
Menacing the tree,
Competent to kill it,
But decoyed may be.
2. Bait it with the balsam,
Seek it with the knife,
Baffle, if it cost you
Everything in life.
3. Then, if it have burrowed
Out of reach of skill,
Ring the tree and leave it, —
’T is the vermin’s will.

section: 397

No station in the day?

Retrieve thine industry.

And art thou sleeping yet?
The lily waiting to be wed,
The bee, dost thou forget?

Thy little plan to me,
Dissuade thee if I could not, sweet,

Is summoned by the tides.

1. Not any higher stands the grave
For heroes than for men;
Not any nearer for the child
Than numb three-score and ten.

The beggar and his queen;
Propitiate this democrat
By summer’s gracious mien.

section: 400
label: Asleep
1. As far from pity as complaint,
As cool to speech as stone,
As numb to revelation
As if my trade were bone.
2. As far from time as history,
As near yourself to-day
As children to the rainbow’s scarf,
Or sunset’s yellow play
3. To eyelids in the sepulchre.
How still the dancer lies,
While color’s revelations break,
And blaze the butterflies!

section: 401
label: The Spirit
1. ’T is whiter than an Indian pipe,
’T is dimmer than a lace;
No stature has it, like a fog,
When you approach the place.
2. Not any voice denotes it here,
Or intimates it there;
A spirit, how doth it accost?
What customs hath the air?
3. This limitless hyperbole
Each one of us shall be;
’T is drama, if (hypothesis)
It be not tragedy!

section: 402
label: The Monument
1. She laid her docile crescent down,
And this mechanic stone
Still states, to dates that have forgot,
The news that she is gone.
2. So constant to its stolid trust,
The shaft that never knew,
It shames the constancy that fled
Before its emblem flew.

His musket on his breast;
Grant, God, he charge the bravest
Of all the martial blest.
2. Please God, might I behold him
In epauletted white,
I should not fear the foe then,
I should not fear the fight.

When what we need is by,

’T is a necessity.
2. Of heaven above the firmest proof
We fundamental know,
Except for its marauding hand,
It had been heaven below.

The foreigner before he knocks
Must thrust the tears away.

Where, keeping house for thee,

And lay the marble tea,

A cycle, it may be,
Till everlasting life unite
In strong society.

Retrospection is prospect’s half,
Sometimes almost more.

The sounds of welcome near!

Our only lullaby.

section: 412

1. Bereaved of all, I went abroad,
No less bereaved to be
Upon a new peninsula, —
The grave preceded me,
2. Obtained my lodgings ere myself,
And when I sought my bed,
The grave it was, reposed upon
The pillow for my head.
3. I waked, to find it first awake,
I rose, — it followed me;
I tried to drop it in the crowd,
To lose it in the sea,
4. In cups of artificial drowse
To sleep its shape away, —
The grave was finished, but the spade
Remained in memory.

section: 413

1. I felt a funeral in my brain,
And mourners, to and fro,
Kept treading, treading, till it seemed
That sense was breaking through.
2. And when they all were seated,
A service like a drum
Kept beating, beating, till I thought
My mind was going numb.
3. And then I heard them lift a box,
And creak across my soul
With those same boots of lead, again.
Then space began to toll

And Being but an ear,
And I and silence some strange race,
Wrecked, solitary, here.

To memory and me.

No more to do have I,

section: 441
label: Retrospect
