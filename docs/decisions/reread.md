# Reread

The first part of synthesis (see [synthesis.md](synthesis.md), still exploratory for essays): going back over a focus's notes one at a time, labelling them as you go, with a scratchpad beside them for rough thoughts. Decided 2026-10-13 and built as a page of its own; since 2026-10-19 it is the notes page (see [foci-and-notes.md](foci-and-notes.md)), the two having overlapped too much.

## The page

**`/foci/:id/notes`, for any focus** (it was `/foci/:id/reread`, which now redirects there), the current one, a past one or an archived one, without making it current. An archived focus's is read-only (see [Archiving](foci-and-notes.md#archiving)). The rail stays the current focus's.

- Every note on a passage in reading order down one column (passage, reference, note: below), then the notes on the focus as a whole under "On the whole focus", each by its number and date ("Focus 2 · October 14"). Each note's labels are pills under it (below). The one you're on is lit and the rest dimmed in place, as clause by clause dims the rest of a passage. The passage is clamped to two lines except on the lit note.
- **Each note is an entry in a commonplace book** (decided 2026-10-27): the passage heads it in full ink (22px on the lit note, stepping down to 19px past about 200 characters or six lines), the reference small under it, then the note (18.5px) as the entry's body. A note on the whole focus has no passage, so its reference heads it. Detached notes are set the same way, without the band.
- **The lit note is a band of paper across the page**, from the rail to the window's right edge (on a phone, screen edge to screen edge), ruled in accent above and below, with a hairline in the rule colour across its whole width between passage and note. No accent bar down its left, no rounded corners. The text keeps its measure: passages to 36rem, notes to 38rem. The scratchpad slides in over the band's right end, as it does over the column.
- **Why:** from 2026-10-25 the passage was set as a quotation (indented, a hairline down its left, 16.5px in a softer ink, under its reference), the note under it at 21px across the full 48em. A short note under it balanced, but under a long one the passage shrank to a grey caption and the note, a block of 95-character lines, looked lost from what it answered. Prototyped 2026-10-27 (not kept): the quotation narrowed to 38rem with a long note stepping down to 18.5px, better but not enough; the passage beside the note in a column of its own, held level with it, which needed a wider page and stacked back into the same problem on a phone; and the passages running as in the reader with each note hung under its passage as an aside, which put the weight on the text rather than on what you'd written. Heading the entry with the passage gave it its weight back instead of shrinking the note. Of the treatments of the lit card, no frame at all lost the note among the others; a gap above and below it, or plain hairlines, did less than the accent rules on a band; a band to the right edge alone looked lopsided until it ran to the left too, without the accent bar. A tint on the passage's half was tried and left out: one paper for the whole entry.
- **Until 2026-10-25** only size and a softer ink had told passage and note apart, and at a glance a passage read as the note's first paragraph. The quotation was prototyped then (not kept) against the passage in italic, which tires over a long passage of prose; on a tinted slip, the clearest but busy down a run of them, even on the lit note only; and a short rule marking where the note begins, which left the passage looking the same.
- **Comparing keeps the commonplace book** (decided 2026-10-27): the other notes are entries as above, at full strength (the note in a softer ink unless it shares a group with the card's note), the lit note's band becomes the card (see [Comparing](#comparing-notes)), and the row at the cursor is a quieter band, ruled in the rule colour rather than accent, so it's never taken for the card's note. For a day the rows kept the quotation under the card, which looked jarringly unlike the page around it.
- **The column is about 48em wide** on a wide screen (decided 2026-10-20), wider than the reader's 34em measure: with the scratchpad put away there's nothing beside it, and comparing notes wants the room (the card's tabs were crowded at 36em, the width it had beside the docked scratchpad). Since 2026-10-27 the notes' text is kept to about 38rem inside it (above), the full width having read too long under a long note.
- `j`/`k` (or `←`/`→`) move, as does clicking a note; a held key moves once, as in the reader. The lit note scrolls to the middle.
- **Where you left off** is kept per focus (`foci.reread_note_id`, cleared if that note goes), so the reread opens there again.
- The way up: Your focuses (› Archived, for an archived focus). Copy as Markdown · Download `.md` at the right of that line, quiet words like the trail, not chips. An archived focus's "Archived October 21, 2026 · Unarchive" follows the count.
- Detached notes follow the run, out of it, to read or discard (see [foci-and-notes.md](foci-and-notes.md)).
- The reference links into the reader; on the lit note it reads "Isaiah 40:25 Open in the reader →", all one link (the hint was once a separate span that lit up with the reference but did nothing when clicked).
- `?label=` rereads only the notes carrying one label: a pill's name, or "Rereading 8 notes ▾" over the run (see [Rereading one label](#rereading-one-label)); `?label=none` only those without one (see [Rereading the notes without a label](#rereading-the-notes-without-a-label)).
- **Editing:** the current focus's lit note gets the reader's editor in place (`e` puts the cursor at its end). A past focus's notes on passages are only read here: the reader's note editor saves to the current focus, and editing them was a nice-to-have left out to keep this simple. Notes on the whole focus save to their own focus, so they can be edited on any focus.

## Notes on the whole focus

See [foci-and-notes.md](foci-and-notes.md) for what they are. Here is where they're written:

- **New note** (`n`), the dashed "+ New note" slip at the end of the run, and on phones New note on the scratchpad's handle (which closes the sheet), open a new row at the end with its editor. (A New note button by the scratchpad went when the scratchpad was put away behind its tab.) The first save makes the note (the reply tells the editor where to save from then on), and it can be labelled once it exists. Moving on from a new row left empty removes it. Clearing a note deletes it, as in the reader. (It was "+ Note on the whole focus", a faint link at the end of the run only: hard to find on a long run, and wordy.)
- **Make a note of this:** select some of the scratchpad and a button over the selection turns it into a note on the whole focus at the end of the run (its lines as paragraphs); "Made a note on the whole focus · Go to it" says so. The scratchpad keeps the working, the note the result. The words stay in the scratchpad.

## Labels

**A user's own labels on notes, like sorting index cards into piles** (many to many: a note can carry several).

- They belong to the user, not the focus (`labels`, unique per user ignoring case, up to 40 characters), so one label can gather notes from several focuses. Another user's labels are a 404, as everything else is.
- **A label needn't have a name.** Notes found to go together (below) make an unnamed label: a group before there are words for it. Naming it later makes it an ordinary label. (Decided 2026-10-19: labelling was hard because it asked for the right word up front, and shades of meaning, "accepting" others or God's will, blurred under one word.)
- **An unnamed label goes by a letter: "group D"** (decided 2026-10-26), the same on every note in it, "group" quiet and the letter a little heavier. Letters are the user's, across focuses, handed out in turn and never reused (`labels.number`, counted by `users.labels_lettered`; A–Z, then AA), so a focus's groups may run D, F, H. A label named later keeps its letter, and has it back if its name is taken away; one named first takes the next when its name goes. It's described by one of its notes, its example (`labels.example_note_id`): the hover title ("group D (like Isaiah 40:6: All flesh is grass…)") and the start of that note on Gather's card. A name is never refused for looking like a letter: "group D" as a name is set as a name, and the letter's setting tells them apart.
- **It was shown "like" one of its notes, 2026-10-19 to 2026-10-26:** "like Isaiah 40:6" and the start of that note, except on the example itself, where it was shown by its next note ("like this note" says nothing). So a group read "like 40:6" on all its notes but one, which read "like 40:8", and the list couldn't be scanned by eye for a group's notes. A number was considered, as the footnotes had (below), but Gather's card numbers groups 1–9 for the moment as keys, and "2 group 7" would have been two numbers for one group.
- A label lasts only while some note carries it: taking off its last note (or deleting that note) drops it. Taking off its example moves the example on to its next note.
- **Naming and renaming** from the band while rereading the label (below), or from the note's groups in **Compare**; a blank name leaves it unnamed again. A name you already have for another label is refused, saying so ("You already have a label called “waiting”"): merging isn't built yet, so the way round is to move the notes over by hand.
- Every note shows its labels as **pills** under it (below), each a way to reread only its label's notes. The lit note has **+ label** (`l`), which opens the picker (below), and **Compare** (`g`). A label is taken off a note from its pill while rereading that label (below), from the labels panel, or in Compare.
- A note set aside as a detached note keeps its named labels' names as text (`detached_notes.label_names`), shown under it after the run; they no longer count anywhere. Unnamed ones aren't kept.

## Labels as pills

**A note's labels as pills under its text** (decided 2026-10-25): named ones A–Z, then unnamed ones ("group **D**"), outlined and quiet in faint ink, without the label colours, which stay in Compare. The lit note's are a little larger. (They had a × to take the label off until 2026-10-26; see below.)

- **They were footnotes, 2026-10-20 to 2026-10-25:** a numbered mark per label after the note's text, in the label's colour, and under the note a line giving each label's name and its other notes by reference, each shown when pointed at. The list of other notes didn't help and made the page wordier, so the pills came back. The numbers had matched the card's group numbers in Compare (then Gather); the buttons on each row already show which groups it's in.
- **Pages for each label are planned** (not decided): each would show every note carrying a label, perhaps across focuses. That's where a label's other notes would be seen.
- **A long reference is cut short in the middle, never at its end** (decided 2026-10-20): where room is short (the card's groups and tabs; until 2026-10-26, a face "like" a note and the cloud) the title or name is cut off with "…" and the last part ("1:6") kept whole, since that's what tells it from its neighbours: "Quick to Help, Slow to Judge… 1:6". The whole reference is the hover title and heads the pop-up; a note's own heading keeps it whole. A user's text with a long title had broken the footnotes' line, wrapping the title centred in its button with the verse alone under it.
- Replies to labelling redraw the pills of every note on the label, since an unnamed label's description (its hover title) moves on with its example.

## Rereading one label

**A pill's name rereads only the notes carrying its label** (decided 2026-10-26), and the count over the run is a chip. The filter had been only in "Rereading 8 notes ▾", which read as a sentence: the user had forgotten it was there. Prototyped (not kept) as three changes, switched on and off against the page as it was; all three were kept.

- **A pill** on any note, lit or dimmed, opens `?label=` on that note (`?at=`, which the reread opens on when it's among the notes shown, saving it as where you left off); hovering it says "Reread only the notes labelled “waiting”". The label being reread is outlined in accent on every note, and its pill leads back to all the notes. Not while comparing.
- **The count is a chip** ("Rereading [8 notes ▾]"), tinted while rereading a label; its menu heads the labels "Only those with the label".
- **A band over the run** while rereading one ("Only the 5 notes labelled “waiting” · Show all 12 ×", tinted as search's "← Back to results" is); ×, Show all or `Esc` (with nothing else to put away) go back to all the notes, on the note you're on.
- **Remove:** while rereading a label, its pill on the note you're on is divided, the right side filled in accent: "( waiting | × Remove )". Removing crosses the pill out, dashed, with **Put back**, and "Leaves the run when you move on" under it; the band adds "· 1 just removed". The label comes off when you move on (the note leaving the run), follow a pill or the menu, go back to all the notes, or leave the page; putting it back before then changes nothing, so an unnamed group's letter and example are never lost to a slip. This replaced the × on the lit note's pills, which would have sat beside a pill that now did something else: removing became a second, deliberate step, on a pill you'd just chosen, and it suits pruning a label one note at a time. The labels panel still takes a label off with a tap.
- **Rename** (decided 2026-10-26): the band has "· Rename" after what it shows ("· Name it" for an unnamed group), or `r`. It opens a field in the band's place, "Only the 5 notes labelled [ waiting ] Save · Cancel", empty for an unnamed group ("Words for group D (optional)"), saying the name changes on every note it's on, under every focus, and what leaving it empty does. Enter saves, redrawing the band, the lede and the pills; Esc leaves it as it was, going no further. A name already taken is refused there, as in Gather. Renaming had been only on Gather's card, on hover, where the user couldn't find it; while rereading a label is when you're looking at what it holds. Prototyped (not kept) with the name itself as the button, dotted-underlined; a plain "Rename" said what it did.
- A label dropped (its last note removed) is a 404 at its old `?label=`, as another user's is: the two can't be told apart without saying which labels exist. Going back to all the notes from the band or `Esc` doesn't go there.
- Considered, for the clash of filtering and taking off on one pill: a menu on the pill (an extra click to filter), taking off from the picker only (hard to find), and keeping × with Undo. The user proposed the divided pill instead.

## Rereading the notes without a label

**"Without a label" in the "Rereading 8 notes ▾" menu** (decided 2026-10-28), under All notes with its count, once any note on the focus has a label. Studying goes in turns: read, write notes, label them, read more, write more; this finds the ones not yet sorted.

- It's `?label=none`: labels go by id there, so a label called "none" is still `?label=17`, and one parameter means one filter, the label or the lack of one.
- Set as rereading a label is: the chip "Rereading [the 4 notes without a label ▾]", and a band "Only the 4 notes without a label · Show all 12 ×" (no Rename: nothing to name); ×, Show all or `Esc` go back to all the notes, on the note you're on. Notes on passages and on the whole focus both; detached notes and "+ New note" left out, as under a label.
- **A note labelled there stays until you move on**, with its new pills, the band adding "· 1 just labelled"; then it leaves the run, as a note removed from a label does. So a slip doesn't take it from under you, and `j` works through what's left until "Every note here has a label now." Not while comparing: Compare's rows are the notes without a label, to group with each other, and those grouped there leave once you're back and move on.
- Considered: a mark on each unlabelled note, or a count in the tallies, to see them without filtering. Left out to keep the page quiet.

## The label picker

**Every label you have as a cloud, to browse as much as to search.** Decided 2026-10-18, after a prototype (not kept).

- All one size, wrapped to fill a picker about 26em wide; the cloud scrolls when there are more than fit, the box and footer staying put. Nothing is cut off: you often browse for a fitting label, more so as they grow numerous.
- **A–Z · Recent** beside the box, remembered in the browser. Recent is when a label was last put on a note (`Label.last_used`, from `note_labels.created_at`).
- Only named labels: an unnamed one can't be typed, and putting a note in one is what **Compare** is for.
- One list: labels used only under other focuses take their place in it, faint, dashed and italic as in the cloud panel, saying where on hover. (They were first a separate group after the rest.)
- Typing narrows it to labels holding what you typed, underlined in each.
- **What Enter does is always said** in a footer ("↵ holiness"), as the user couldn't tell whether Enter took the first match or made a new label of the part typed. Enter takes the closest match, lit in accent: starting with what you typed, then a word starting with it, then holding it, the first among equals; `↓`/`↑` or pointing move it. With nothing typed nothing is lit, and Enter does nothing. A new label of what you typed, unless it's one of yours already, waits in the footer ("+ New label “ho”"): a click, or `↑` round from the first label, and Enter takes it on its own when nothing matches.
- **Placed to stay in sight:** under + label unless that leaves room for only a row or two of labels and there's more above (on phones the scratchpad's handle takes the foot of the screen); then over it, the box at its foot nearest the label. The cloud is cut to the room there is. + label scrolled out of sight is brought back to the middle first. (It used to open under + label regardless, often partly below the fold.)
- An early fix showed only the 8 closest labels with "and N more"; dropped, as it stopped the browsing.

## The labels in use: cloud and tallies

So you can see the shape of the synthesis forming. **A panel called up over the foot of the scratchpad** ("Labels" at the right edge, or `c`) and put away again (×, `Esc` or `c`), not always on screen. With the scratchpad put away, it comes up on its own, over the foot of the screen at right.

- **Cloud** (the default): each label a pill, so a label of several words reads as one; set in the reading face, sized by how its count compares with the labels' average (a geometric mean): 16px at the average, 5px more for each doubling above it and 5px less for each halving below, between 11px and 34px, so labels used equally are all 16px whatever their count (decided 2026-10-28; it was 15–32px against the most-used label, which drew equal labels all at full size and left the least-used barely smaller); alphabetical, so a label grows in place. The lit note's labels are filled in accent. Labels used only under other focuses trail at the end, faint, dashed and italic (12px, at the small end), to take up here too.
- **Tallies**: each label with its count and a row of marks, one per note in reading order, filled where the note carries it (the lit note's mark taller), so you see where in the focus a label gathers.
- Pointing at a label lights every note that carries it in the column; tapping one puts the lit note on it, or takes it off.
- The view chosen is remembered in the browser.
- Unnamed labels are in both, by their letters, after the named ones (in the order they were made). This is to see how it feels, and may be walked back.
- A tag cloud was first ruled out as feeling like a productivity tool; that was Claude's caution, not the user's, and doesn't hold.

## Comparing notes

**Grouping by recognition rather than by naming:** "do these two go together?" instead of "what's the word for this?". Decided 2026-10-19, after prototypes (not kept). Its button, first "Find what goes with this", was renamed Gather on 2026-10-20 as too long and awkward, keeping `g`. **Renamed Compare on 2026-10-28**, with "Hold this" and the like becoming "Compare with this": two words (gather, hold) were in use for one thing, holding a note up to compare the others against, and neither said it plainly. `g` stays, as `c` is the labels.

- **A mode of the reread, not a page:** `g`, or **Compare** on the lit note, turns the column into the comparison; `g`, `Esc` or the card's × turns it back, on whichever note you ended on. `Esc` puts away the scratchpad or the labels first, if they're out.
- **The note you're on is held up at the top of the column** on a card, sticky: its band, as on the page, from the rail to the window's edge, ruled in accent, the tabs cut into its top rule after a quiet "Comparing", "Done comparing ×" at the right. Its groups (its labels, named or not) numbered for the moment, in the order they were made, a line each: its face (its name, or "GROUP D" and the start of the note it's described by), the notes in it ("with 40:6, 40:8", each showing its note when pointed at, with **Compare with this**; on a touch screen a tap shows it and a second puts it away; Esc puts it away; a reference to a note not shown under `?label=` says so), and on hover **name it / rename** and **remove**.
- **Up to three notes to compare with at once, under tabs along the card's top** (decided 2026-10-20): each tab its reference and first words, the one you're on open onto the card and the last to give up its words when they're crowded, × at the right; `‹ 3 of 12 ›` at the right of the note's reference on the card (it was in the tab row, crowding it). **Compare with this** on a row (or `Enter` on the row at the cursor, or from a note shown on the card) puts that note under a tab of its own, at the end; with three, the one used longest ago, never the one you're on, gives way. The row of a note under a tab says "Comparing · go to its tab". A tab (or `[`/`]`) goes back to its note, and the list to where it was when that note was last the one you were on, the cursor too. `x` or a tab's × puts one away; putting away the last leaves. `J`/`K` step the note under the tab, past those under the others. Leaving and coming back keeps the tabs (for the page's visit, not stored).
- **Comparing with another note doesn't move the list:** the row that becomes the note you're on leaves the list and its neighbour takes its place on screen; the note you left comes back to its place. (It used to scroll to the top, so linking two notes seen while on a third meant leaving, finding the one, coming back and finding the other.)
- **Every other note of this focus under it**, in reading order, each with its labels as pills, then on their line a button per numbered group (filled where it's in it), **+** for a new group of the two, and **Compare with this**. `j`/`k` move through them (the row moved to is scrolled clear of the card), `1`–`9` put it in a group or take it out, `+` (or `n`) makes the new group. Only this focus's notes; linking across focuses is for later.
- **Always grouped from the card's side:** you choose among its groups, never the other note's, so a note already in several just gains one more. The question "which of its groups?" doesn't arise.
- **Pointing ties the two together:** a number on a row lights its group in the card; the groups the row at the cursor is already in are ringed, and its reference picked out (in the group's colour) among each one's notes; a group pointed at in the card lights its notes in the list. ("40:4 is in it" said so in words at first, which distracted more than it helped.)
- **The card is kept as short as it can be, without scrolling** (a scroll bar inside a sticky card confused): the card's note is a line of passage, its reference (with `‹ 3 of 12 ›`), a hairline and a line of note (decided 2026-10-27), then "In 3 groups" and its groups, a line each. You know the note you're comparing with; what you're looking for is what's like it, so it needn't be in full before you. When either line is cut off, a click or tap on it (or `p` or `o`) lays the whole entry over the card: passage, reference, note. Only a click, not hover; a click anywhere, `Esc`, moving the cursor or changing tabs puts it away, `Esc` before it leaves the mode. A group's face cut off opens over what's below on hover or a tap, the whole text laid exactly over the cut one. (Until 2026-10-27 the passage was a line and the note three, each opening on its own on hover; the card was the tallest thing on a phone.)
- **A row's passage, cut to two lines, opens the same way** (decided 2026-10-26): a click on it (which also makes it the row at the cursor), or `P` on the row at the cursor, lays the whole passage over the cut one, so the rows below don't move; a click anywhere, `Esc`, moving the cursor or changing tabs puts it away. Only a click, not hover, so pointing down the list doesn't keep opening them. The rows had stayed cut while gathering, the reread opening only the lit note's, which Compare hides.
- **Colours:** each group has its own muted colour (`label.hue`, its id mod 6, `--g0`…`--g5`), on its number and buttons here only.
- Replies redraw the chips of every note on the label (an unnamed label's description moves on with its example as notes come and go), the cloud and the tallies; the card and buttons are drawn again from them.

## The scratchpad

**One per focus** (`has_rich_text :scratchpad` on Focus): freeform and unlabelled, for half-thoughts, things to compare, sentences being rewritten. An autosaving Lexxy editor.

- **Put away by default, behind a tab at the right edge** (decided 2026-10-20), with the Labels tab under it: "Scratchpad" (or `s`, which also puts you in it) slides it in over the right of the page, full height; × or `Esc` puts it away. Room is the constraint while grouping, and in practice notes on the whole focus did the work the scratchpad was meant for (written so they can be grouped with other notes); the scratchpad matters more later, when starting to write something whole. It was docked at right, always open, until then.
- On phones it's still a bottom sheet whose handle shows its first line, with New note and Labels on the handle; with the sheet shut, the labels panel comes up as its own short sheet resting on the handle, so the notes stay in view.

## Ways in

It's the notes page, so the ways in are the notes page's: "Notes on this focus" in the rail and the count on each focus's card. (Until 2026-10-19 there were separate ones: Reread among the notes page's actions, `r`; "· Reread" on the cards; "Reread these notes" in the menu.) No prompt to reread from the reader: the app doesn't nudge.

## History

Prototyped 2026-10-13 in `prototype/reread/` (since removed): A, the reader one level up (the note where the passage stands, the scratchpad docked); B, index cards with the labels as piles on the desk; C, every note in one spotlit column; D, the scratchpad as the page with notes fed in from the side. B's piles showed the shape of the labels, which the user liked most, but C's way of reading won; variants of C added the labels as a sticky strip (C1), a grid in the margin (C2), tallies under the scratchpad (C3), and a cloud on a panel called up over the scratchpad (C4). **C4** was chosen, its tallies kept as the panel's second view. Its first `L` for the panel clashed with `l` for labelling, so the panel is `c`.

"Find what goes with this" (now Compare) was prototyped 2026-10-19 in `prototype/groups/` (not kept), all four on one set of notes: A, two columns (the held note at left, every other note at right); B, a mode of the reread, the held note on a card over the column; C, piles shown by an example note, the held note weighed against the piles rather than each note; D, notes as cards on a table, dragged about and ringed into overlapping groups. D was too busy and awkward to move about, worse on a phone; C struggles past a handful of piles; A took too much room. **B** was chosen as the middle way. Its card first described groups only by their notes ("with 40:6, 40:8"), which didn't say what a group was about, so they gained faces; its card first scrolled inside itself, so its parts were cut short to open over what's below instead.

Compare (then Gather) was prototyped again 2026-10-27 (not kept), after the notes page became a commonplace book and its quoted rows and boxed card looked jarringly unlike it: A, the card as the held note's band with its rows as entries, the group buttons after their pills; B, the same with the card's groups standing in the right margin beside the note and each row's buttons level with its passage in the same margin, a ledger; C, A with the held note cut to a line of passage and a line of note, the whole entry popping up over the card. The user liked B's margin but phones and tablets would have put it under the note anyway; **C** was chosen.

Linking two notes seen while on a third was prototyped 2026-10-20 in `prototype/groups/` (not kept): E, any row made the held note without the list moving, with a way back; F, a row picked up into a tray at the foot, the rows grouping with it while the held note stayed; G, E's holding with three held notes under tabs, the scratchpad put away, and labels as footnotes. **G** was chosen, after the user proposed its tabs, footnotes and hidden scratchpad (the footnotes went back to pills on 2026-10-25): F put a second note's groups on screen, and room was the constraint.
