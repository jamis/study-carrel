# Discovery (exploratory)

> **Tentative. Nothing here is decided.** These are notes from a brainstorm (2026-10-07 to 2026-10-09) to come back to. Don't build from this file, and don't treat any of it as a rule, until it has been settled and moved into the index as a decision.

## The problem

Study Carrel serves three purposes: **discovery** (finding something to study), **study** (the reader, which works well) and **synthesis** (distilling what the notes taught you). They could form a loop: something stirs a question, you study under it, and synthesis leaves loose threads that seed the next focus.

Discovery matters most for a **new user**, who signs up, is shown the new-focus form, and may have no idea what to ask. It also matters for an **experienced user** between foci, who wants a new question but doesn't have one yet.

## The idea so far: exploring as a focus

A meta focus such as "What should I study?" or "What speaks to me right now?" is a perfectly valid focus. Under one, the goal is exploration rather than notes: flipping through the library with Random, as you'd flip through a book, and keeping what stands out as seeds for future study.

Ideas floated:

- **Two ways in on the first screen:** "I have a question" (today's form) or "I want to look around" (creates an exploring focus such as "What speaks to me right now?" and opens the library). At the least, the form's examples should include a meta question.
- **Flipping as the main action.** Random moves from the rail to beside the arrows ("Turn to another"). The "Another" chip was dropped on 2026-10-05 because its meaning was unclear; under an exploring focus it's plain. Random flips, the arrows settle in to read around a passage. Narrowing the scope (the existing ▾) deserves a more visible place.
- **Notes hidden entirely while exploring** (the user's idea), replaced by a prominent keep section in the right-hand page or bottom sheet:
  - top: Keep for this passage, then the remark ("What caught you?");
  - below: what you've kept while exploring, newest first, each with "Start a focus from this".
  - Keeps become the only record of an exploration, so the 200-character remark limit may need revisiting.
- **The kept passages as seeds.** After a few keeps, perhaps one quiet line: "You've kept 6 passages. Is one of them a question?" No counts or progress bars.
- **Prose landings.** A lone random sentence (Montaigne especially) can land flat out of context; exploring might open with the ¶ view showing.

## Curated lists

The user likes a few specially curated lists to explore by: e.g. Bible, Christian, Sacred texts, Philosophy.

- A list is a selection of **passages**, not works; a collection is everything on the shelf, a list is someone's choice from it.
- An entry may span units ("Isaiah 40:28–31", "Walden: Where I Lived ¶16–17"): you land on the first, the rest a step away.
- Maybe written as text files beside the corpus (a citation plus an optional line on why), giving reviewable diffs, with the loader flagging citations that don't resolve.
- Random over a list would be another scope, balanced across works.
- A newcomer's first few rolls might come from a curated list, so the first impression isn't a genealogy or a purity law. Alternatives considered: skipping list-like units by heuristic, or accepting strangeness as part of flipping.
- Later, perhaps: users make their own lists (a set of keeps is halfway there).
- Possible names: reading lists, anthologies, florilegium.

## Names

"Seed tray" captures the idea but doesn't suit the study carrel metaphor. Alternatives from places where reading happens:

- **Commonplace book**: the reader's notebook of copied-out passages that struck them.
- **Gleanings / gleaning**: gathering what's left in the field after harvest (Ruth); "Go gleaning" for the exploring mode.
- **Hold shelf**: books held for you to collect later (utilitarian).
- **On the desk**: the pile beside your carrel.

Claude's preference was "Commonplace" for the collection of keeps and "gleaning" for the mode. Not chosen.

## Open questions

1. **Is exploring a focus or a place?** Under one-focus-at-a-time, starting a study focus ends the exploration, but an experienced user will want to return between studies. Options:
   1. A focus like any other, archived when replaced (archive fills with "What speaks to me?" foci).
   2. One permanent exploring focus per user to switch to and from, with the study focus set aside, not archived (Claude leaned this way).
   3. A mode that works under any focus (cleanest for wandering mid-study, but exploring stops being a question).
2. **Is a focus required at all** to read, or can someone just read?
3. **What makes a focus "exploring"**: a flag the user chooses, or something inferred?
4. **Curated lists**: who writes them, how many, and how they relate to collections.
5. **AI**: could help (e.g. drafting a focus from a set of keeps), but raises privacy and tone questions. Not discussed for discovery specifically.
