Reader layouts

Four takes on a calmer reading view. All keep the large passage, the focus as it is, and an always-open note; none preview neighboring verses or show the circle strip.

ADesk

One short line on top: the focus on the left, “Isaiah 40”, recent places and ⋯ on the right. Passage and an unboxed note column side by side, divided by a hairline. Verses step with ‹ 3 of 31 ›; the number opens a plain number grid.

BOpen book

No app bar. A two-page spread on the desk: running head and folio like a printed book, the passage on the left page, a faintly ruled notebook page on the right. On a phone the notebook follows the passage on one page.

CSingle column

The focus, the passage, and your note directly under it, nothing more. The reference opens a contents list (each verse by its opening words). The page margins turn the page; the menu and arrows fade out while you read or write.

DRail and ruler

Tools in a thin icon rail on the left, the note as a full-height docked page on the right. Position in the chapter is a hairline ruler (taller tick = has a note) in place of the numbered circles.

ERail and steps

D’s layout (tool rail, docked note, no top bar) with A’s navigation: ‹ 3 of 31 › under the passage, the number opening a plain number grid.

¶Prose by sentence

E, reading prose one sentence at a time. A paragraph’s first sentence opens with a ¶ hung in the margin; references read “Self-Reliance 5:3” (paragraph 5, sentence 3). The number opens sentences grouped by paragraph, and ¶ (or p) shows the whole paragraph. Tabs on the left switch between Thoreau and Emerson, and between sentences and paragraphs.

j/k or ←/→ step · n write · b keep · Esc leave the note. The tab on the left edge switches layouts and keeps your verse.
