Study Carrel

Pose a question. Focus your study. Gain insight.

Study Carrel is your personal desk in a quiet library. It surrounds you with poetry, prose, and sacred texts. No distractions. All the time in the world. This is your space to focus on finding what you seek.

How does it work?

How does it work?

Begin your session by stating your focus: a question, metaphor, or thesis that you want to investigate.

And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.Jeremiah 29:13

Select a work to study—Study Carrel includes a number of public domain works of poetry, prose, and scripture. Find one that speaks to you.

Books are the treasured wealth of the world and the fit inheritance of generations and nations.Henry David Thoreau, Walden

Read it one paragraph, stanza, or verse at a time. The rest of the text fades away, keeping you focused on what you're studying.

This, what is it in itself, and by itself, according to its proper constitution? … Thus must thou examine all things, that present themselves unto thee.Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Record your insights. Your notebook is always right there, ready for you to jot down what occurs to you.

Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it.Habakkuk 2:2

Later, review the thoughts you noted.

Looking back is best that is left,
Or if it be before,
Retrospection is prospect's half,
Sometimes almost more.Emily Dickinson

Change your study focus at any time, or jump back to a previous focus and pick up where you left off.

What works are currently available?

Study Carrel includes a list of public domain works that were selected for their potential to reward a slow, careful reading. Today the corpus contains:

Sacred Texts

  • The Bible, King James Version: the complete Old and New Testaments
  • The Book of Mormon (public-domain text)
  • The Dhammapada (Müller translation)
  • The Tao Te Ching (Legge translation)
  • The Bhagavad Gita, as The Song Celestial (Arnold translation)
  • The Quran (Rodwell translation)

Poetry

  • Emily Dickinson, Poems (all three series)
  • Robert Frost, Collected Poems (1930)
  • Edgar Allan Poe, Poems

Prose

  • Henry David Thoreau, Walden
  • Marcus Aurelius, Meditations (Casaubon translation)
